#4471 says "one issue, one fix queued". The reports merged into it say otherwise. Grouped by what can actually break a date for that tenant, not by subject line, they fall into three problems. Each needs its own fix, so each now has its own ticket.

The queued parser fix closes only the first problem. The other two stay broken after it ships.

The three problems

parser — the shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so any day ≤ 12 comes out with day and month swapped: 7 April becomes 4 July. It reaches people through the app, email, and the booking-v2 form. This is the fix that is queued.

hint — booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. Day-first tenants on booking-v3 type what the hint tells them, month first, and the form then reads the date the tenant's way. The symptom is the same swap, so subject lines and intake tags cannot tell it from parser; only the channel and the tenant's form can. Fixing the parser does not touch the hint.

one-day — dates move by exactly one day. "Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May." "Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday." A month-first read cannot move a date by one day, so these are not parser at all. They point at time zones: a date handled as midnight in one zone and shown in another. The desk had already noticed them (desk-notes.json) but counted them under parser.

How to tell them apart

  • Ignore meta.intakeTag. It is a guess from the reporter's browser and disagrees with the tenant-based cause on dozens of these reports.
  • Day and month swapped, reported through a booking-v3 form → hint. Any other way in → parser.
  • The date moved by one day, or two people saw different weekdays for one meeting → one-day, whatever the tenant.
